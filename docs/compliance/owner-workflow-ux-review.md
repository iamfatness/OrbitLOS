# Compliance UX review for brokerage owners

November 12, 2026 · Live OrbitLOS Worker `d9b16326-ff29-4ef1-b020-f632e6cdcce8` · Isolated build `a5f383b`

## What an owner needs from Compliance

A brokerage owner starts with the office, the person responsible and the work that needs attention. Orbit currently starts with the type of record. A renewal, file finding, monthly task and filing workpaper each have a working interface, but the owner has to know which interface holds the next step. For Align Lending, the first screen offers a license register, a monthly review and a research library. The branch setup lives in Team and the Mortgage Call Report lives in Reports. In the fictional pilot, the first screen instead shows nine peer destinations and an overview of sample findings.

The most valuable next release is a branch-aware Compliance home with a single view of actionable work. Keep the license register, exact loan links and versioned evidence behind that home. Those existing parts are where the owner verifies details and records decisions.

## First-time owner walkthrough

The owner knows the company, office, staff and work they do. Orbit can guide setup from those facts. The interface should surface the difference between a missing record, a possible requirement and a requirement that someone has verified for this brokerage.

| When | What the owner should see | What the owner does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First sign-in | **Set up Compliance** with a short checklist. “Start with the company and office you manage.” | Add or select the company, choose the branch and confirm which states and activities it handles. Add existing license records or assign someone to find them. |
| After setup | **Check what applies** with plain-language items. Each item explains why it appeared, which entity and branch it refers to, and who should review it. | Confirm the business facts. Send unresolved applicability questions to the responsible reviewer with the relevant source and exact record attached. |
| Each morning | **Needs attention**. For example, “North Office branch license expires in 7 days. Assigned to you.” | Open the exact license, record the renewal evidence or assign follow-up, and return to the same branch and queue. |
| At month or quarter end | **Prepare this period’s review**. The page shows work waiting on evidence, ready for internal review, and waiting for a filing receipt where applicable. | Open the workpaper or related file, review the supporting records, and record the outcome and next step. |
| During a review or exam | **Find evidence** by office, loan or period. | Open or export the exact version, see who recorded it, and identify what is still missing. |

Use plain names at entry points. “Company and branch details” can lead to the saved brokerage scope. “What we may need to do” can lead to applicability review. “Recurring work” can lead to obligations. “Why this appeared” can open the source and the conditions behind it. Retain precise legal terms on the evidence screen and explain them where a decision depends on them.

In the first-use test, give a person unfamiliar with mortgage compliance a fictional brokerage and no written walkthrough. Ask them to identify the next setup action, locate an upcoming branch renewal, route an unresolved filing question, and find what would count as proof it was submitted. Observe where they stop, which labels they misread and whether they can tell an internal target from a verified filing date. A release candidate passes when they can complete those tasks from the interface and correctly explain what is still awaiting review.

## Layout and navigation to prototype

- **Compliance home** opens the same way for owners in operational and fictional workspaces. Show workspace context once and identify pilot-only actions where they appear. An empty operational workspace opens with the next setup action. A populated workspace opens with actionable work and a compact next-30-days view.
- Use four work areas beneath the home: **Licenses & branches**, **Calendar & filings**, **File reviews**, and **Policies & evidence**. Keep **Research & sources** one click away from the relevant record and reachable from the home. Use the existing list, exact-record and return patterns.
- At phone widths, put the branch selector and the first actionable item ahead of secondary navigation. Show the complete selected branch, entity or requirement below a selector when the control truncates the label. Keep one primary action per task and give repeated actions specific names.
- Use a consistent status vocabulary: **Needs setup**, **Due soon**, **Past internal target**, **Awaiting evidence**, **Awaiting review**, and **Reviewed** where the underlying record supports it. Use **Submitted** and **Accepted** only when there is evidence for those distinct events.

## Release order and acceptance

1. **Owner home and setup.** Reuse existing data for the first release and expose only work the role can see. Test a fresh office and an owner with two branches, one missing license record, one upcoming renewal and one open monthly item. The phone landing must show a meaningful next action within its first viewport. The owner must be able to open the exact record, return to the same branch and filter, and recover from an interrupted load.
2. **Close the reporting loop.** Verify saved scope to obligation to period to assigned review to matching report or evidence. The owner should reach the planned item from the confirmation without re-entering entity or month. Change a license after a period is recorded and confirm that history retains the original basis and the next period exposes the change.
3. **Finish evidence retrieval and staff follow-through.** Verify owner and assigned-staff boundaries across two branches and two brokerages. Retrieve an older review, the relevant loan evidence and an outstanding policy acknowledgment from the correct context. Exercise two same-name loans, no eligible files, unavailable owner, read-only history and a stale edit.

Use a fictional two-branch owner rehearsal and a read-only Align Lending check before publishing each cycle. Confirm keyboard focus, phone reflow, specific loading and recovery messages, and scope preservation when the owner returns from a record. Preserve the separate legal review and provider gates.

## Broker owner first-use acceptance

Use a fictional brokerage with two branches, an upcoming renewal, a saved state awaiting review, an assigned policy and a report period with a recorded filing confirmation. Give the owner the prompts below without showing them the navigation. Use a phone as well as a desktop. Ask the owner to explain their answer in their own words before helping.

| Prompt for the owner | Observable outcome |
| --- | --- |
| “You opened a second office. Record what you already know about the company, the office and the states where you do business. What still needs someone to verify?” | The owner can identify a missing record and a saved business fact awaiting review. A branch without a license record prompts a review question. The record count alone conveys no decision about which licenses apply. |
| “Find what North Office needs to do next. Tell me who owns it and what date matters.” | The owner opens the exact renewal and can tell that the branch selection scopes renewals while internal reviews remain brokerage-wide. Returning preserves the selected branch and the action's keyboard focus. |
| “You’re unsure whether the brokerage needs to file something for a state. Send the question to your reviewer and find their reply.” | The owner assigns it from the saved state, selects a supporting source and chooses an internal follow-up date. The reviewer opens the exact saved version from Needs attention. The owner finds the status, decision or next action, and evidence reference beside the saved state. |
| “Check whether the quarterly report was actually filed. Show me the proof you would save.” | The owner distinguishes an internal target, a prepared report and a recorded external filing confirmation. They can retrieve the original confirmation even after a newer status is recorded. |
| “Find a staff policy acknowledgment and the supporting record for a reviewed loan.” | The owner can identify the responsible staff member, open the policy version, and retrieve the relevant loan evidence. In a workspace where sample tools are absent, observe whether the owner can tell which evidence workflows are available. |

**State review handoff.** On the local candidate, **Assign a question for review** is available on a current saved state record. The work is assigned to an active brokerage admin in Monthly review with the exact company, saved state version, facts and selected source. Historical versions retain their questions and decisions. An internal follow-up date is a brokerage planning date. Keep filing preparation and submission evidence distinct. NMLS describes the Mortgage Call Report filing step in the company user's Filing tab. [NMLS Mortgage Call Report](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/Products/nmls/pubs/policyGuide/reference/policyGuide/maps/topics/nmlsPG_MCR_subsection-1.html)

The owner-session prompts are a release usability check. The scripted walkthrough uses fictional records and cannot establish that a first-time owner can complete the same tasks unaided. Counsel review remains a separate production release gate.

**Superseded loan findings.** The local pilot shows the original package and its status with an **Open current evidence** action. It reloads the loan and puts the latest sample disclosure package first. **Back to finding** returns to the original case and its evidence action. The general loan action is omitted from these two focused views. The original package receipt and case history remain available.

## What an owner should be able to explain

- **Setup.** “I recorded the company, offices, states and licenses we have on file. I know who will review which requirements apply.” A branch with no license record and a state with saved business facts awaiting review should each prompt a different conversation.
- **Daily work.** “I can see the license that expires next, who owns the follow-up and what date matters. Our internal review dates are brokerage planning dates.” The branch selector applies to renewals. The internal review queue covers the brokerage.
- **Reporting.** “We prepared the report and recorded the confirmation we received from the filing system. I can find the original receipt using its saved location.” Filing history identifies the latest status staff recorded and retrieves older confirmations by their exact number. The **Download Orbit record** action exports the staff entry. A missing original receipt location is visible on the entry.
- **Review and evidence.** “I can send a question from the saved state record and find the reviewer's response. I can follow up on a policy acknowledgment and open the version behind it.” Loan evidence still lives under its own pilot workflow. Observe whether a first-time owner can find it using the prompts above.

The next release check is an unaided session with an owner who has limited compliance experience. Start with the two-office fixture and the prompts above. Capture where they hesitate and have them explain the setup, internal date and filing evidence in their own words. Counsel review of applicability is a separate release check.

## First-time owner release findings

The owner should be able to bring three things to Orbit: what the brokerage does, the records it already has and the questions it needs answered. The first release should give each question an owner, an internal follow-up date and a place to record the answer. The [CFPB compliance management review](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/supervision-examinations/compliance-management-review-examination-procedures/) informed the focus on ownership and follow-through. The [NMLS Company Dashboard guide](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/Products/nmls/pubs/ugCompState/licensing/company/topics/licCompMaint__licStatusCompDash_determining.html) informed the distinction between the company, branch and originator records and the source of external filing status.

| Priority | Owner friction on the local candidate | Observable outcome for the release |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | An owner asking “Which filings should we review?” had to choose from 21 saved sources before submitting. In the single-admin fixture, the only reviewer choice was the owner. | Let the owner save the question with the company and state already attached, even when the source and reviewer are unknown. Show who will follow up, including a route to record a response obtained outside Orbit. Keep the eventual applicability decision and its evidence separate from the question. |
| 2 | A branch selection scoped renewals and survived opening and returning from a license. Reloading restored All branches. The Branch label also looked like a filter for the whole page. | Label the renewal filter by what it affects, remember the choice in that brokerage after refresh, and keep brokerage-wide review work clearly labeled. Show the owner whether each state is recorded, awaiting a review decision, or needs another look after the underlying facts changed. |
| 3 | The renewal action opened the correct record in a general Edit license form. A historical filing search showed an earlier Submitted entry, the latest recorded Accepted status and a location for the original receipt. | Put the renewal action and evidence needed at the top of the selected license. In filing history, show the status of the matching confirmation and the latest recorded status on separately labeled lines. Make a missing original receipt location actionable. |

Run the first-use session without a tour. Ask the owner to open a new office, hand off an unresolved question, work a renewal and show the original filing evidence or explain what is missing. A prompt that needs the facilitator to supply a regulation, pick a reviewer, or explain what a status means is a workflow gap. Use the owner's explanation to judge whether the screen communicates what is known and what still needs review.

**Question-first follow-up.** The updated local candidate lets an owner save a question from the current state record with an internal follow-up date and an accountable brokerage admin. The current owner is selected by default. A source is optional. The follow-up opens in Monthly review with the original company and state details. The owner can record a decision or next action and a reference to an answer received outside Orbit. The completed work stays visible on the state record. The next owner workflow change is to preserve the renewal branch filter after refresh and label the work it affects.
