# Mortgage reporting research: Alabama–Missouri

Research date: 2026-11-11. Status: **preliminary research; user review and lawyer approval required before launch**. This document covers 25 states, not the entire federal/state compliance inventory. Michigan is the pilot. No production entity, license inventory, fiscal year, or reporting authority has been verified. A source-backed finding is not legal approval, tested platform support, or proof of filing.

## Reading the matrix

**NMLS baseline (N)**: NMLS describes MCR reporting for state-licensed companies and companies employing state-licensed MLOs. Residential Mortgage Loan Activity (RMLA) is quarterly, within 45 days after calendar-quarter end. Financial Condition (FC) is quarterly for lenders/servicers and annually within 90 days after calendar-year end for brokers. FC is company-level; state activity is reported in the applicable state sections. State-specific supplemental requirements and license applicability still need verification. [NMLS policy](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/Products/nmls/pubs/policyGuide/reference/policyGuide/maps/topics/nmlsPG_MCR_subsection-1.html).

**N is a common research baseline, not evidence that a state's additional requirements have been cleared.** “Unresolved” means the platform must show a research gap, not assume no obligation. Unless explicitly stated, zero-activity treatment for the state-specific report remains unverified. Do not generate a zero return just because no loans funded: applications, withdrawals, denials, pipelines, servicing, and company financials may remain reportable.

Use MCR Form Version 7 for applicable 2026 periods; historical amendments require the period's schema. The current guide supports manual entry and XML upload. This research does not establish a direct filing API. [FV7 guide](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/Products/nmls/pubs/mcr/index.html), [March 2026 release overview](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/Products/nmls/aboutNMLS/Shared%20Documents/NMLS%20Release%20Overview%20March%202026.pdf).

## Michigan pilot: candidate obligations and acceptance criteria

DIFS treats annual financial statements and NMLS FC as different submissions. Annual statements must follow GAAP; the FAQ does not require an audit. The cited authorities are MBLSLA §7(2) and SMLA §6a(2) for statements; MBLSLA §21(3) and SMLA §6a(13) for FC special reports. MCR activity replaced the old annual activity forms and informs operating fees. [DIFS first-mortgage FAQ](https://www.michigan.gov/difs/news-and-outreach/faq/consumer-finance/1st-mortgage). DIFS separately states Michigan mortgage licensees/registrants and companies employing Michigan licensed MLOs file quarterly MCR. [MLO FAQ](https://www.michigan.gov/difs/news-and-outreach/faq/consumer-finance/mortgage-loan-originator). The licensing hub links financial-statement instructions, MCR/FC guidance and FIS2053. [Mortgage licensing hub](https://www.michigan.gov/difs/industry/licensing-cf/mortgage).

The following are proposed product controls, not additional legal conclusions:

| Candidate ID | Proposed behavior | Required before enabling live use |
|---|---|---|
| MI-MCR-RMLA | Track each calendar quarter, state activity, review, submission receipt and amendments | Verify legal entity/NMLS ID, relevant license/registration dates, sponsored MLOs, zero-activity rule, FV7 state fields and deadline adjustment rules |
| MI-MCR-FC-BROKER | Annual company FC based on calendar year; do not create one duplicate FC per state | Counsel confirms broker-only classification; reconcile accounting totals and annual period |
| MI-MCR-FC-LENDER-SERVICER | Use quarterly FC when the company's covered activity requires it | Detect table funding, correspondent lending, servicing and classification changes; counsel resolves mixed activity |
| MI-ANNUAL-FS | Separate annual financial-statement task based on fiscal year, with document evidence | Confirm covered license(s), fiscal year, GAAP preparation, uploader category, FIS2053 applicability and attestation |
| MI-OPERATING-FEE | Preserve MCR-to-assessment traceability and invoice evidence | Verify actual assessment instructions; no invented annual fee due date |

Required pilot tests: a non-calendar fiscal-year broker gets distinct FC and financial-statement deadlines; a lender/servicer receives quarterly FC; zero funded loans do not erase application reporting; license acquisition/surrender mid-quarter goes to applicability review; amended source data preserve submitted versions; missed deadlines remain overdue until resolved; export generation does not set “filed”; absent counsel approval blocks launch. Use fictional profiles until real brokerage details are supplied.

## Legal review and research closure checklist

1. User reviews scope and workflow, then an identified lawyer reviews rules and training content before launch. Record reviewer, date, scope, rule version, sources, qualifications and unresolved exceptions. User review is not lawyer approval.
2. For every state obtain the current NMLS license checklist, regulator instructions and applicable statute/rule. Verify supplement selection, zero returns, fiscal/calendar basis, due-date arithmetic, weekends/holidays, submission location, signatory, CPA/audit requirements, renewal and surrender tails.
3. Preserve distinctions among broker, lender, correspondent/table-funder, servicer, processor, exempt employer, company and individual MLO. Evaluate legal entity, property location, loan purpose/lien, activity and license dates; state selection alone is insufficient.
4. Resolve identified conflicts: Alabama substitution/legacy terms; Alaska old form; California separate licensing regimes; Colorado fiscal-year FC wording; Connecticut expired 2026 relief; Delaware volume dates; Illinois annual-report substitution scope; Kansas FV7 supplemental mapping; Missouri equivalent-report acceptance.
5. Close explicitly unresolved states and fields above before calling them supported. A sourced regulator directory earns “source located,” not “rules complete.” Do not turn a missing deadline into an invented default.
6. Distinguish monthly internal reconciliation and readiness from statutory quarterly/annual filing. Track event-driven notices and conditional federal reports separately; this matrix does not establish a universal monthly mortgage-broker return.
7. Counsel-approved rules require evidence-backed implementation tests, restricted preparer/reviewer/submitter permissions, tenant isolation, audit history, export verification and actual receipt/rejection tracking before operational acceptance. Maintain historical schemas and rule effective dates.
8. Recheck sources before launch and after regulatory changes. Store retrieved date and archived source evidence alongside rule versions; stale or conflicting evidence should reopen review rather than silently remain approved.

No launch approval, regulatory filing, deployment, or complete 50-state certification is conveyed by this research.
