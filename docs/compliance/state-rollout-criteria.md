# 50-state compliance rollout

All 50 states are in delivery scope. Michigan is the first pilot. The 50-state catalog and reporting matrix are preliminary research. Completion is measured per state, legal entity, license, activity, product and effective period. The user reviews each release scope and a lawyer reviews it before regulatory use.

An admin in a workspace with Compliance enabled can open **Compliance → Research & gaps → State rollout**. Select a state and open a requirement review or control gap for licensing, reporting, loan terms and disclosures, communications, or records and examinations. Existing research cases retain their source snapshot, owner, internal follow-up and history. The counts show how many areas have tracked work. Each area can contain multiple legal obligations, and tracking alone does not establish their applicability or implementation.

Start in **Brokerage → First-day checklist** to open branch setup and state licensing. If Compliance is off, the licensing step opens workspace settings to enable it. Use **Compliance → Licensing** to select or add a legal entity and record its state licenses. The register stores the company, branch or loan officer holding the license, recorded status, optional effective and expiration dates, and an optional evidence reference. It shares entities with Monthly review. Set up branches in **Team → Branches** and officers in **Team → Staff access**. Archived branches and changed staff access leave a review flag on the retained license record. **Renewal review** shows active records expiring in the next 30 days and active or expired records with a past expiration date. The brokerage dashboard shows the same renewal attention across the workspace’s legal entities and opens the exact license. A recorded date change clears the dashboard item when the license leaves the window. Admins can assign a renewal owner, record a note and evidence reference, and inspect the 25 most recent changes from the license. Assigned renewals appear under **Today → My Tasks** and count toward that admin’s Today badge. An assignment linked to a former admin access revision remains in history and is flagged for review on the brokerage dashboard. The register supports internal review without driving loan eligibility or filing.

In the register, select a state to review company, branch and loan officer records for the selected legal entity. In **Lead Inbox → Work lead**, an admin selects a proposed lead owner and opens **Check state licenses**. The check shows company, proposed loan officer, and relevant branch record counts per legal entity. It identifies a managed branch recorded at intake, includes the proposed officer’s current branch assignments, and flags when the officer has no current assignment at the intake branch. Archived branch records and prior staff access revisions are flagged for review. The shared inbox selection shows company and managed intake branch records. Open a single matching record directly, or view the records for that entity, holder and state. Returning to the lead preserves the proposed owner and selected review state and refreshes the counts. The optional property state from website quote requests and realtor referrals preselects the check and carries into the draft 1003. An admin can select a different state for the check. That choice applies only to the check. An admin or the assigned LO can use **Correct property state** on an open lead. The reason and old and new states appear in Lead activity and carry into the loan history at conversion. Fictional campaign and CRM CSVs accept an optional property state column. The CRM export includes the saved state and lead history. The check uses staff-entered statuses and dates and is advisory.

For each proposed state scope:

1. Record the entity, exact licenses and sponsorship, allowed activities and products, regulator and allocation of broker and lender duties. Resolve unknowns with the compliance reviewer.
2. Extract each applicable requirement and exception with its authoritative section, effective dates, facts, responsible party and evidence. Capture counsel's applicability decision for the stated scope.
3. Implement and test each control or document a reviewed operational procedure. Include an in-scope success, missing facts, exceptions, changed source versions and a boundary case for timing or eligibility.
4. Rehearse reporting, records, supervision and examination evidence for that scope. Track filing receipt or correction evidence where applicable.
5. Obtain the user's and lawyer's review of the exact supported scope. Record verification and production availability for that release. Keep unsupported combinations visible.

The reporting starting points in `state-reporting-review-sources.json` preserve all 50 rows from the November 11 draft reporting matrices. They retain the source links and unresolved questions. The other review areas use the existing state authority references and prompts for further rule extraction. Research must be refreshed and checked against the particular license and period. Expand **Specific requirements** under the applicable review area. Each entry preserves the authority link, check date, evidence needed and a question for counsel. Admins can assign the specific requirement or control gap independently. The catalog is in [state-rollout-requirements.json](state-rollout-requirements.json).

Under **Brokerage scope for counsel review**, choose a legal entity already created in Monthly review. Record activity, lien scope, company and office authority, originator sponsorship, products, responsibility and reporting period as evidence references or explicit unknowns. Save a version to bind those facts to the entity and the state's current source snapshots. Reopen or download that exact version when preparing the counsel packet. Later versions preserve the original, and source or entity changes are flagged. Recording a packet reference leaves the scope pending counsel review. Scope evidence is stored per brokerage, entity and state and is protected from ordinary restore rewind. Local implementation is pending browser acceptance.

Michigan counsel decisions include entity and license scope, first and secondary mortgages and individual exceptions, MCR and Financial Condition scope and zero-activity handling, the separate fiscal-year financial statement, and record retention and examination evidence. The fee and advertising reviews now ask counsel to confirm charges and rate guarantees, the TRID and compensation overlap, and campaign and product scope. Remaining state-specific loan terms, disclosures, communications and examination controls still need review and operational verification.
