# State compliance research: Montana through Wyoming

Research baseline: November 10, 2026. Scope: OrbitLOS independent wholesale mortgage brokerage, LOS/POS/PPE and CRM; expansion into table funding, non-delegated lending, servicing, commercial/DSCR, reverse, HELOC or independent processing requires a separate applicability decision.

**Status: preliminary primary-source inventory; all 25 states require legal review before production approval.** The controls below are proposed engineering requirements, not evidence that Orbit implements them. A regulator page or current-looking search result is not a complete statute survey. Sources were searched for every state; main sources were opened except Tennessee, where direct access failed. Supplemental blocked/indexed sources are explicitly identified. Review date is retrieval date, not a representation of legal effectiveness. Numeric limits are intentionally omitted where the operative text, exceptions or current adjustment could not be verified.

For every state, the unresolved scope includes financial-privacy/data exemptions, breach notification, unfair/deceptive practices, fair lending, telemarketing/texting/DNC, call-recording consent, electronic signatures, retention/legal holds, complaints, accessibility, corporate/branch licensing, bonds/net worth, MCR/state reports and license-renewal changes. Silence below does not mean exemption. Both borrower residence and property/staff/business locations may matter. Federal duties remain additional.

Each entry’s source supports the stated baseline only. **Confidence:** moderate for narrow statements from directly retrieved operative text or regulator guidance; low for completeness, historical guidance, blocked sources and open items. No state is counsel-approved or release-ready on this evidence.

## Montana (MT)

The Montana Mortgage Act (MCA Title 32, chapter 9) distinguishes brokering, lending and servicing. The regulator’s broker guide includes lead generation and independent processing among broker activities. It specifies five years of file retention measured from last activity and advertising identification/NMLS website linking.

**Platform controls:** State-scoped company/MLO permissions; last-activity retention clock; immutable disclosure signatures and delivery; marketing footer with approved name, NMLS ID and Consumer Access link.

**Open review:** The guide says disclosure within three business days, while the regulator’s [mortgage page](https://benefits.mt.gov/BFID/mortgage-consumer-finance/mortgage) quotes statutory disclosure at application before services. Resolve against current MCA 32-9-124 and ARM 2.59.1737 before enabling a deadline. High-cost, fees and privacy overlays remain unverified.

Primary baseline: [Montana official source](https://eld.mt.gov/BFID/mortgage-consumer-finance/MBguide).

## Nebraska (NE)

Nebraska calls both lending and brokering “mortgage banking.” NDBF states compensated arranging, origination and related mortgage activities require licensing or registration; most entities obtain a mortgage banker license. This is not evidence of nationwide permission from a single company license.

**Platform controls:** Store Nebraska company/branch/MLO credentials separately, expiry and verification date; route files only to authorized staff. Include a refinance benefit-review evidence field; NDBF publishes a [net tangible benefit form](https://ndbf.nebraska.gov/industries/mortgage-lenders).

**Open review:** Establish the exact benefit-test scope, current Residential Mortgage Licensing Act sections, exemptions, permitted fees, record period/trigger, disclosure forms, advertising and privacy requirements.

Primary baseline: [Nebraska official source](https://ndbf.nebraska.gov/industries/mortgage-lending-license).

## Nevada (NV)

Nevada MLD identifies NRS/NAC 645B for mortgage companies/originators and NRS 598D as the unfair-lending overlay. Escrow and servicing are separately regulated. The regulator’s index establishes authorities, not a validated numerical high-cost test.

**Platform controls:** Distinguish mortgage company, originator, servicer and escrow authority. Require a Nevada fee/product policy before pricing approval; retain brokerage agreement, advertisements, lender submissions and fee changes.

**Open review:** Read operative NRS/NAC 645B provisions, NRS 598D applicability and current amendments; resolve disclosure timing, fee caps/refunds, compensation, retention, privacy and breach obligations. No production “passed” result should be inferred from this source index.

Primary baseline: [Nevada official source](https://www.mld.nv.gov/industry-info/statutes-and-regulations/).

## New Hampshire (NH)

RSA 397-A covers business conducted from New Hampshire as well as loans secured by New Hampshire real estate. It requires company and individual licensing, with MLO effectiveness linked to employment/retention. Section 397-A:16 requires a written explanation of purpose/disposition of fees collected before closing; rate locks must comply with commissioner rules.

**Platform controls:** Evaluate staff/business location and collateral, not only property ZIP; retain advance-fee explanation before collection and lender-backed lock evidence.

**Open review:** Determine branch/remote-work rules, lock details, recordkeeping clock, high-cost/interest overlays and privacy/marketing duties. Resolve each exemption with evidence rather than a user-selected unchecked exemption.

Primary baseline: [New Hampshire official source](https://www.gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXXV/397-A/397-A-mrg.htm).

## New Jersey (NJ)

New Jersey has a Residential Mortgage Lending Act licensing regime and a Home Ownership Security Act overlay. The DOBI high-cost outline is historical and describes several loan categories; it cannot serve as a current executable threshold table. [DOBI licensing guidance](https://www.nj.gov/dobi/banklicensing/rmla/qiresmortbroker.html) separately addresses the qualified individual.

**Platform controls:** Record entity/MLO/qualified-individual authority. Version high-cost calculations and include broker compensation, financed fees and product features in review evidence. Retain signed service agreement where required.

**Open review:** Current NJHOSA definitions, adjusted thresholds, repeals/amendments, broker liability and exemptions; current service-agreement/disclosure rules, fee limits, retention, marketing and privacy. Do not encode the old outline literally.

Primary baseline: [New Jersey official source](https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_banking/hoa03outline.htm).

## New Mexico (NM)

NMRLD states mortgage loan companies and covered branches require licensing. Its FAQ identifies mortgage loan summary/compensation disclosures at least two days before closing and rate-lock disclosures. The [mortgage industry page](https://www.rld.nm.gov/financial-institutions/who-we-regulate/mortgage-industry/) identifies a periodically published Home Loan Protection Act percentage rate.

**Platform controls:** Capture lock date and terms; require compensation and summary delivery evidence before closing; use effective-dated high-cost input tables and preserve the table version.

**Open review:** Reconcile older GFE/TIL wording in Rule 12.19.8.12 with current TRID implementation. Validate business-day definition, receipt semantics, product coverage, fees, retention, privacy and current high-cost values before automation.

Primary baseline: [New Mexico official source](https://www.rld.nm.gov/financial-institutions/about-us/faqs/).

## New York (NY)

DFS distinguishes registered mortgage brokers, licensed mortgage bankers and separate MLO requirements. DFS’s [cybersecurity resource center](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/cybersecurity) identifies Part 500 coverage for DFS-authorized entities and amended requirements; exemptions require an entity-specific analysis.

**Platform controls:** Track registration type and sponsorship; evaluate Part 500 coverage/exemptions with evidence; support MFA, access reviews, audit evidence, incident escalation and annual compliance artifacts. Require current New York disclosure/lock and high-cost review packages before release.

**Open review:** Current 3 NYCRR Part 38, Banking Law 6-l/6-m applicability, fee and retention rules, cybersecurity category/exemptions, incident deadlines, financial privacy, advertising and special reverse/VA counseling disclosures. A general platform security claim does not establish Part 500 compliance.

Primary baseline: [New York official source](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/apps_and_licensing/mortgage_companies).

## North Carolina (NC)

NCCOB requires licensing for covered residential brokering under Chapter 53 Article 19B, with qualifying-individual and branch requirements. [G.S. 53-244.109](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/PDF/BySection/Chapter_53/GS_53-244.109.pdf) requires reasonable efforts toward an advantageous loan and disclosure of reasonably known compensation across options.

**Platform controls:** Validate company, MLO, branch and qualifying-individual authority; retain scenario alternatives and selection rationale. Export complete application histories and the state loan-level reporting fields described by [NCCOB](https://nccob.nc.gov/financial-institutions/mortgage/mortgage-licensing-information).

**Open review:** High-cost/rate-spread and anti-flipping rules under Chapter 24, current thresholds, reporting schema/deadlines, record retention, privacy, and marketing restrictions; prove disclosure responsibilities in lender contracts.

Primary baseline: [North Carolina official source](https://nccob.nc.gov/financial-institutions/mortgage/licensing-information/mortgage-lender-broker-licensing-requirements).

## North Dakota (ND)

A 2023 DFI notice moved residential mortgage lenders into NDCC Chapter 13-12 and established branch registration. Older general money-broker FAQs therefore cannot determine residential scope alone. Money brokering and individual MLO activity also require classification under their respective chapters.

**Platform controls:** Store the precise license regime and activity instead of a single “ND licensed” flag; evaluate borrower residence, collateral and commercial purpose. Route uncertain broker-only and table-funded cases to review.

**Open review:** Read current Chapters 13-04.1, 13-10 and 13-12 and classify independent wholesale brokerage; verify disclosures, fees, record periods, advertising and privacy. Do not automatically migrate a broker’s authority based solely on the lender transition notice.

Primary baseline: [North Dakota official source](https://www.nd.gov/dfi/sites/www/files/documents/Regulatory%20Guidance/North%20Dakota%20Legislation%20Notice%202023.pdf).

## Ohio (OH)

ORC 1322.07 requires company registration for the principal and branch offices and individual MLO licensing, subject to applicable exceptions. [ORC 1322.45](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1322.45) imposes good-faith/fair-dealing and advantageous-loan efforts.

**Platform controls:** Verify registration at the responsible office; retain loan alternatives, advice and compensation; require product/fee review for Ohio loans. Preserve actual communications and explanations rather than a checkbox declaring “best interest.”

**Open review:** Current Chapter 1322 administrative rules, broker agreements, fees/advance payments, high-cost and prepayment restrictions, retention clocks, advertising, breach/privacy and recording-consent requirements. Lending authority and broker authority must be validated for each business model.

Primary baseline: [Ohio official source](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1322.07).

## Oklahoma (OK)

The Department of Consumer Credit lists mortgage lender, broker and MLO licenses separately. Its [duties page](https://oklahoma.gov/okdocc/about/duties.html) also identifies UCCC oversight, including rates, charges, terms, disclosures and advertising.

**Platform controls:** Capture exact activity/role and applicable license; classify each fee and financing purpose; preserve compensation and disclosure evidence. Maintain a rule-version date so updated SAFE Act rules do not silently alter past results.

**Open review:** Retrieve enacted 2026 administrative changes and current Title 59 SAFE Act/Title 14A UCCC applicability. Validate fee ceilings, high-cost treatment, record retention and privacy. The short regulator license descriptions are not complete statutory definitions.

Primary baseline: [Oklahoma official source](https://oklahoma.gov/okdocc/licenses-we-regulate.html).

## Oregon (OR)

ORS Chapter 86A establishes mortgage banker/broker and MLO licensing, records/inspection obligations and disclosure requirements. Section 86A.169 refers to federal lending disclosures; its legacy citation text must be reconciled with current federal implementation.

**Platform controls:** Track banker/broker activities separately; maintain retrievable application, pricing, disclosure and advertisement versions; require a state-reviewed product policy before approving high-cost or unusual products.

**Open review:** Current administrative rules, Oregon high-cost provisions outside Chapter 86A, fee and lock requirements, state privacy applicability/exemptions, breach notification, telemarketing and recording consent. Determine whether a DSCR/business-purpose loan changes state licensing coverage rather than assuming federal exemptions govern Oregon.

Primary baseline: [Oregon official source](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors086a.html).

## Pennsylvania (PA)

Pennsylvania requires mortgage-business licensing under Chapter 61; the regulator lists conduct rules in Chapters 46–49 and servicing rules separately. Its [2014 policy letter](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/dobs/documents/interpretive-letters/mortgage-licensing-act/140116_one-page%20mortgage%20disclosure%20discontinued.pdf) concerns discontinuation of the Pennsylvania one-page disclosure, despite older rulemaking that describes it.

**Platform controls:** Preserve lender/broker disclosure responsibility and actual delivery evidence; version templates by effective date; require current policy verification before adding that legacy form.

**Open review:** Confirm continuing effect of the 2014 policy and subsequent changes, Chapter 46 conduct standards, Consumer Equity Protection Act/high-cost coverage, fees, recordkeeping, privacy and advertising. Do not treat a superseded disclosure as a live automated requirement.

Primary baseline: [Pennsylvania official source](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dobs/non-bank-licensees).

## Rhode Island (RI)

230-RICR-40-10-2 applies to covered lenders, loan brokers and MLOs. It requires records from initial contact through final disposition, including application status dates, disclosures, adverse action and lock confirmation. It also contains remote-work controls.

**Platform controls:** Preserve CRM solicitation and attempted contact history through conversion; record the Regulation B completeness date separately from submission; retain lender lock confirmations and office identity; enforce remote access safeguards.

**Open review:** Current Chapter 19-14 licensing details, Rhode Island Home Loan Protection Act/230-RICR-40-10-3, fee and disclosure requirements, retention duration, privacy/breach, marketing and call-recording requirements. Do not delete a nonconverted lead merely because no loan was funded.

Primary baseline: [Rhode Island official source](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/part/230-40-10-2).

## South Carolina (SC)

SCDCA’s broker page expressly includes correspondents, table funding and independent contractor processors/underwriters. It requires an annual report and mortgage log and references a 2024 interpretation permitting APOR substitution for a retired high-cost benchmark.

**Platform controls:** Determine SCDCA broker versus separate lender authority; retain processor classification; produce mortgage-log and reporting evidence. Version threshold inputs and record the legal interpretation authorizing a benchmark.

**Open review:** Current Chapter 40 broker contract/compensation disclosure requirements, High-Cost and Consumer Home Loans Act, exact benchmark formula, fee caps and retention. Reconcile legacy GFE language before generating forms; review privacy, advertising and consumer preferred-provider requirements.

Primary baseline: [South Carolina official source](https://consumer.sc.gov/business-resourceslaws/licensing/mortgage-broker).

## South Dakota (SD)

South Dakota distinguishes a mortgage brokerage license for fee-based placement from lender/servicer licensing. The regulator also identifies a nonresidential mortgage lender license, so commercial purpose is not automatically a licensing exemption.

**Platform controls:** Classify brokerage versus lending/servicing and residential versus nonresidential activity; verify MLO supervision and license status; retain renewal and company-change evidence.

**Open review:** SDCL 54-14 and ARSD 20:07:19 operative disclosure, fee, record and advertising obligations; distinguish Chapter 54-4 money-lender rate rules from mortgage rules. Resolve high-cost applicability, privacy/breach rules and any remote-work or branch requirements before state release.

Primary baseline: [South Dakota official source](https://dlr.sd.gov/banking/mortgage_licenses/default.aspx).

## Tennessee (TN)

TDFI’s indexed mortgage-company guidance cites Tennessee Code 45-13-201 licensing under the Residential Lending, Brokerage and Servicing Act and includes compensated arranging and table funding. Direct retrieval of official TDFI and Secretary of State rule pages failed with 403/timeouts in this research session; this entry has lower source-verification confidence.

**Platform controls:** Store lending/brokering/servicing permissions independently; attach lender authority to locks and fees; maintain application, compensation and disclosure history.

**Open review:** Obtain full current statute and Rule 0180-17, verify the lock agreement requirements and all fee/high-cost/retention rules; assess privacy, breach and outreach overlays. Search-index excerpts do not authorize executable deadlines or a compliance pass.

Primary baseline: [Tennessee official source](https://www.tn.gov/tdfi/mortgage-consumer-lending/mortgage.html).

## Texas (TX)

The January 2026 SML guide requires accessible transaction logs, marketing, compensation agreements, vendor contracts and complete loan files; brokers must retrieve documents from lender portals. It identifies home-equity, home-equity refinance, reverse and wrap-loan documents and third-party processing/underwriting logs.

**Platform controls:** Classify Texas homestead/home-equity status and refinance history before pricing; prevent generic closing approval for unreviewed special products; archive executed lender documents and processing logs.

**Open review:** Texas constitutional Article XVI §50 timing, fee/LTV and location restrictions; current Finance Code/TAC and SML versus OCCC jurisdiction; secondary loans, disclosures, retention, privacy, telemarketing and recording. Do not reduce Texas home-equity compliance to a cash-out flag.

Primary baseline: [Texas official source](https://www.sml.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/mortgage-compliance-guide-v12.pdf).

## Utah (UT)

Utah DFI expressly explains that DRE primarily regulates retail closed-end first-mortgage origination/brokering. A DFI residential first-mortgage notification or DFI MLO license alone does not authorize those activities; other products and servicing can fall under Title 70D.

**Platform controls:** Store agency and license type, lien and open/closed-end product; reject permission substitution. Track principal lending manager and approved company/trade name through [DRE company guidance](https://commerce.utah.gov/realestate/mortgage/mortgage-companies-dbas-and-branches/).

**Open review:** Current Title 61-2c, R162-2c records/security/disposal, Title 70D and fee/disclosure requirements, high-cost treatment, privacy and marketing. The rule PDF was indexed but direct retrieval failed; no numeric retention period is certified here.

Primary baseline: [Utah official source](https://dfi.utah.gov/non-depository/mortgage-lending/).

## Vermont (VT)

Title 8 Chapter 73 governs licensing and distinguishes brokering, MLO and loan-solicitation activities. [DFR broker rules](https://dfr.vermont.gov/reg-bul-ord/mortgage-brokers), available through indexed official text but blocked on direct opening, prohibit a broker issuing locks/commitments in its own name and identify a financial-privacy overlay.

**Platform controls:** Validate licensed location/sponsorship; render locks in the lender’s identity; retain approved broker agreement and compensation evidence; require a financial-information-sharing basis before realtor or marketing exports.

**Open review:** Retrieve full current B-2014-02 and privacy B-2018-01, agreement approval requirements, fee/interest/high-cost limits, retention, advertising and lead-generation scope. No assumption that federal GLBA rules exhaust Vermont privacy duties.

Primary baseline: [Vermont official source](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/fullchapter/08/073).

## Virginia (VA)

Virginia Chapter 16 governs company mortgage lender/broker activity, with consumer-purpose one-to-four-family collateral definitions and conditional remote-location treatment. Individual MLO rules are in [Chapter 17](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title6.2/chapter17/section6.2-1701/); independent processors require separate analysis.

**Platform controls:** Match role, property/purpose, company and individual authority; record office/remote authorization. Preserve lock/fee commitments and broker advice. Review paid referral arrangements before commission attribution becomes payment.

**Open review:** Current 10VAC5-160 including disclosures and advertisements, permissible fees and broker duties, high-cost overlays, records, privacy and breach notices. Confirm 2026 amendments and effective dates rather than using the older consolidated PDF.

Primary baseline: [Virginia official source](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title6.2/chapter16/).

## Washington (WA)

WAC 208-660-430 requires covered disclosures within three business days of application or receipt of third-party-service funds. If the lender discloses, the broker must retain both the disclosures and the agreement assigning that responsibility. [DFI examination guidance](https://dfi.wa.gov/mortgage-brokers/mortgage-broker-examinations) describes three-year retention after last activity/use.

**Platform controls:** Trigger tasks from application and money receipt; require actual lender disclosure artifacts and agreement; archive communications, advertising and changed locks with last-use retention.

**Open review:** Business-day/receipt definitions, current form exceptions and federal overlap, broker versus Consumer Loan Act license scope, high-cost/fee limits, privacy, telemarketing and recording consent. Federal retention may require longer than the state baseline.

Primary baseline: [Washington official source](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=208-660-430).

## West Virginia (WV)

West Virginia’s 106CSR5 rule effective May 1, 2026 differentiates recordkeeping by lender/broker and closed/unclosed disposition. For unclosed brokered files, the excerpt specifies 36 months from the latest application, credit document, required disclosure or consumer termination request. The [Secretary of State](https://sos.wv.gov/licensing/other-registrations/credit-service-organizations) identifies potential additional CSO registration/disclosure obligations for loan brokers.

**Platform controls:** Preserve role-specific retention triggers and cancellation evidence; evaluate CSO duties independently; store broker contract and borrower notices.

**Open review:** Complete rule/stated exceptions, closed-loan periods, Chapter 31-17 fees and product restrictions, CSO exemptions/cancellation, privacy and outreach. Apply the longest applicable retention, including federal requirements and legal holds.

Primary baseline: [West Virginia official source](https://apps.sos.wv.gov/adlaw/csr/readfile.aspx?DocId=58849&Format=PDF).

## Wisconsin (WI)

Wisconsin DFI says a mortgage banker must separately obtain broker authority before brokering. Third-party processing/underwriting companies also require licensing and an employed Wisconsin MLO. The regulator’s [statute index](https://dfi.wi.gov/Pages/FinancialServices/MortgageBanking/StatutesRules.aspx) identifies Chapters 224, 428 and 138 and DFI-Bkg 40–46.

**Platform controls:** Require activity-specific credentials; do not inherit broker permission from banker status. Retain signed broker agreement, compensation disclosures and evidence supporting borrower-interest duties.

**Open review:** Current agreement requirements and [MB302 form](https://dfi.wi.gov/Documents/FinancialServices/MortgageBanking/Forms/MB302.pdf) versus legacy GFE wording, fees/high-cost/interest rules, records, privacy, breach and advertising. Review contractor processing permissions before allowing production handoffs.

Primary baseline: [Wisconsin official source](https://dfi.wi.gov/Pages/FinancialServices/MortgageBanking/FAQs.aspx).

## Wyoming (WY)

The Banking Division’s official FAQ describes first-lien residential mortgage activity under the Residential Mortgage Practices Act and junior liens under the UCCC, potentially requiring a supervised-lender license. Its discussion dates to the 2005 regime and must be checked against current statutes.

**Platform controls:** Capture lien position and financing purpose; evaluate mortgage versus UCCC authority independently; keep licensing evidence, fee terms, agreement and disclosures with the file.

**Open review:** Current Act/UCCC definitions and exemptions, MLO/branch rules, advance-fee and lock terms, high-cost/interest restrictions, recordkeeping, privacy and advertising. Historical FAQ coverage is a research lead, not a validated 2026 product-eligibility decision.

Primary baseline: [Wyoming official source](https://wyomingbankingdivision.wyo.gov/mortgage-lending/general-information-press-releases).

## Required acceptance evidence before a state is enabled

1. Counsel/compliance owner approves applicability by legal entity, activity, product, borrower/property/staff locations, with statutory citations and effective dates.
2. Required documents have approved templates, sender/responsible party, trigger, calendar definition, delivery/receipt semantics, change/redisclosure and waiver rules.
3. Fee/high-cost/interest tests have verified current tables and positive, negative and boundary test cases; unknown inputs block the affected decision.
4. Company, branch and MLO permissions have verification provenance, expiry, sponsorship and revocation handling; a stored NMLS number alone is insufficient.
5. Retention policies cover record class, trigger and longest applicable period, with legal holds and export/retrieval after vendor termination.
6. State outreach/privacy matrix is separately researched and approved before live campaign sending, recording, sharing or deletion.
7. End-to-end tests prove intake, pricing, lender submission, disclosure delivery, closing and export enforce approved rules server-side. Manual reviews identify reviewer, evidence, timestamp and specific rule; no generic “compliant” override.
